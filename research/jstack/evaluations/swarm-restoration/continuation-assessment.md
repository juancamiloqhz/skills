The remaining ambiguities are narrow:

- A valid BLOCKED coverage result has no explicit continuation procedure. S:37 permits BLOCKED reports; S:43 says each required slice needs "a result"; S:47 expressly names dropout and exhausted evidence retry as incomplete coverage. Those sentences do not explicitly classify every BLOCKED report. The declared done predicate therefore matters. If BLOCKED means the required work was not performed, calling the whole mixed task complete would be unsupported. If the defined task was to report readiness or blockers, an evidenced blocker may satisfy that different predicate. The text does not prescribe an automatic retry count for BLOCKED.
- First-pass cancellation has a clear completion prohibition but no timeout, escalation schedule or maximum wait. S:47 expressly provides draining when cancellation is unavailable. It does not define when an available but unsuccessful or unconfirmed cancellation becomes unavailable. Until actual terminal state and outputs are reconciled, this omission cannot justify a completed result or a replacement writer.
- The generic dropout instruction at S:39 says to proceed with N-1, while S:47 qualifies what can be claimed for each shape. Neither gives an automatic fresh-task retry for ordinary dropout. That differs from the explicit one-retry rule for missing SHA/method evidence. Replacement is not a mandated continuation for D or E.
- Rank all requires all "required" valid results under S:29 and S:47. E assumes the dropped arm is one of those required by the declared ranking. If the frame explicitly excluded an optional arm from the ranking, these two files do not say that its absence invalidates the required ranking.

These findings establish what the written instructions require. They do not establish that the runtime successfully enforces cancellation, output isolation, catalog validation or model inheritance.
